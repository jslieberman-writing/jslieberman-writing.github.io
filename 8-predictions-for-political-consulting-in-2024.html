8 Predictions for Political Consulting in 2024

Jordan Lieberman

Jul 17, 2025
09/27/2023

The fourth quarter of 2023 is upon us, which seems like the right time to consider what next year has in store for political and public affairs consulting. Here’s a preview of the next 12 months:

1. Integration of AI Tools to Make Us Better at Our Jobs

AI has quickly become a valuable tool for candidates and causes, but it doesn’t replace creativity or project management. Consultants are now better equipped to analyze data and extract actionable insights, enabling more targeted and personalized outreach.

AI-driven tools are improving voter modeling and optimizing text and visual messaging. But let’s be clear: these tools exist to make us better at our jobs — not the other way around.

2. Accelerating Supply Path Optimization

Supply path optimization (SPO) is gaining real momentum in political advertising. We flagged earlier this year that programmatic platforms would reorient toward SPO, and that prediction has already proven accurate.

In 2024, expect a more refined SPO landscape with streamlined bidding, better cost control, and increased delivery efficiency — all of which will continue pushing DSP consolidation.

3. Consolidation Among Demand Side Platforms (DSPs)

DSP consolidation is reshaping the political advertising ecosystem. Power is shifting away from technology platforms and back toward advertisers and buyers.

Consultants will need to track how consolidation affects pricing, data access, and bidding dynamics. MediaMath won’t be the last DSP to fail, and Xandr won’t be the last platform to exit political ads.

4. Margin Erosion for Larger Firms

Larger political consulting firms will face increasing margin pressure in 2024. Lower barriers to entry and growing competition from smaller, more nimble firms are making scale less protective than it once was.

5. Lower Expense Account Spending

Selling in 2024 looks nothing like 2019. Expense accounts are the quiet casualty of COVID and remote work, and that trend will continue.

Zoom meetings and gift baskets have replaced steakhouse lunches. As much as I miss PJ Clarke’s, I can do without the 6 a.m. Acela.

6. The Linear TV Death Spiral

Linear TV continues its long decline. In 2024, consultants must lean further into addressable platforms, streaming, social, and digital media.

These channels offer superior targeting and are essential for reaching younger voters with tailored messaging.

7. Public Affairs Spending Holds

Public affairs spending should remain strong, but election chaos remains a wildcard. Insurrection is bad for business — far worse than recession or regulation.

8. Retirements Accelerate

Because of rapid industry growth beginning in the 1980s, retirements are accelerating. New entrants are far more diverse than the generation they’re replacing.

I was called a greybeard for the first time this year. Four kids and a new business may have accelerated that moment — but here we are.

Jordan Lieberman is the CEO of Powers Interactive, a programmatic media company.
