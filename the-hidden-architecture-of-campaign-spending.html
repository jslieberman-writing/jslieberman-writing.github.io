Turns out, it's just Breakfast Club with more money.

We built Cerebro for the least understood corner of Washington, tracking over 2B data points, to confirm what every 22-year-old campaign volunteer already knows: it’s not what you know, it’s who you sat next to at the tiny training institute in Arlington. And once you see the patterns, you can’t unsee them.

I would tell my younger self that college barely mattered: attend Leadership Institute or its Democratic equivalents, run a campaign, get a job at a party committee, and spend nights making friends with people who don’t look like you. A few decades later, the data gives us a roadmap for becoming the 8% of political campaign vendors who survive more than three cycles.

Networks, Not Markets

Political consulting does not operate as a competitive marketplace. It operates as a networked economy. Using 54M+ federal campaign finance transactions (2004–2024), state filings, and other public data—roughly 2B records—we can quantify those network effects.

If you can navigate this world, your company thrives. If not, most firms reset after two cycles. If you were expecting a meritocracy, you made a wrong turn.

Why Printers Outlive Digital Shops 3-to-1

High-Retention Infrastructure (>70% retention)

The Utilities: Compliance & Legal
With ~68% survival over two cycles and 42% over three, this is the only category with meaningful bipartisan overlap. The top five firms capture 60% of spending. Regulatory expertise creates a moat—and firing your lawyer is inconvenient.

The Infrastructure Kings: Direct Mail & Print
Print firms show ~65% survival over three cycles—three times higher than digital specialists. Giant machines that print paper create natural barriers to entry. These firms survive through multi-cycle relationships, not mass marketing. They know who to have a beer with at the Pollies.

The Numbers Priests: Polling & Research
Polling firms with committee alumni last four times longer. The top ten firms capture 45% of revenue. This moat will erode as founders retire and tech pressure mounts.

Medium-Retention Professional Core (40–70%)

The Establishment Guard
Democratic establishment shops and top-tier Republican performance marketers survive through alumni pipelines that reinforce the core thesis of this analysis.

The Border Crossers
Corporate and issue-advocacy hybrids survive by diversifying revenue. Geographic specialization beats ideological purity. They don’t do Q1 layoffs—but they know the best steakhouse in Tallahassee.

High-Churn Categories (<40%)

Cannon Fodder: Digital Operations
Digital consulting is brutal. Survival drops to single digits over three cycles. Thirty percent of dead firms reappear under new LLCs. Resurrection is a feature, not a bug.

Feast & Famine: Fundraising & Field
Only 6% survive three cycles. COVID accelerated a collapse already underway. Even though canvassing still works, nobody answers the door anymore.

The Moat Is Tiered

Client relationships become durable once spending crosses a local threshold: ~$100K in small states, ~$250K federally, $500K+ in large states. Below that, firms churn. Above it, relationships behave like annuities. The win isn’t the sale—it’s the toothbrush.

Alumni & Mafias

About 70% of senior staff at the top 50 vendor firms served at national committees. Committee alumni firms show 2.5–2.7× higher survival and nearly triple revenue growth. It’s not the company—it’s the people.

Leadership Institute alumni show 2–3× higher survival odds. On the left, the New Organizing Institute briefly played a similar role, but its legacy faded faster.

Ghost Hydras & Lineages

When MSHC dissolved, its alumni seeded a $100M+ successor ecosystem. The firm died; the network lived.

Arthur Finkelstein’s lineage functions like an underground root system—rarely visible, deeply binding campaigns across continents. His DNA still shapes modern Republican politics.

Geography Is Destiny

Some states burn vendors every cycle. Others entrench them for decades. California operates like a utilities board. Florida thrives on churn. Some states export over 80% of their spend out of state.

Extinction Events

Obama-era digital killed telemarketing. COVID reshaped field. AI will wipe out button-pushers next. If a robot can replace you, it will.

Basically Breakfast Club

The ecosystem is tribal. Most firms live in one orbit. The survivors bridge multiple. Diversity is insulation.

It’s Music, Not Chaos

What looks like fragmentation is a self-sorting system. Vendors that last are wired into networks that route money, talent, and opportunity. Seeing the music through the chaos is the real advantage.
